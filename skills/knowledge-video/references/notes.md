# Observations on copy and pacing

Seen in tested videos and in well-liked explainers. Use them, or do the opposite; they are not rules.

- **The first two seconds decide the swipe.** Openings that were tried: a multiple-choice question on screen from frame 0; a quick flash of counter-intuitive facts ("the company that played Go produced two Nobel laureates") and then a rewind to the start. A warm-up like "here's a quiz" loses people
- **Tell it as a story**: names and years; for a mechanism, follow one concrete thing all the way through
- An everyday comparison, a twist, and an ending about the viewer are common among well-liked explainers
- **Without narration viewers only read**: one line per screen, about 20 Chinese characters (8–10 English words) reads well; about 0.6 s plus 1 s per 6 characters on screen (Netflix's limit for Simplified Chinese subtitles is 9 characters per second)
- **The whole video should feel like it's going somewhere**: the author called a version where every scene used the same template "flat". A thread through the whole video (a timeline, a list that keeps filling up), pacing that speeds up toward the end, and a question planted at the start that pays off in the middle and the end all make people keep watching
- Key lines on the music's changes: reveals and twists on the first beat of a bar that suddenly gets louder, suspense where the music thins out

## Seen 10-05 / 10-06 (two hits taken apart, then *Fish and Bear's Paw*)

- **Topic**: 7 of the top 8 videos in Douyin's #vibe知识大赏 weren't about AI (luck, accidents, blind dates, fate, the Silk Road…); the one AI video won by being first. What the top ones share is an everyday question everyone already has an answer to, answered by one hard idea. A video on how AI works inside (word vectors) was fun, but we judged it couldn't reach the top
- **Skip a step in the reasoning and viewers fall off.** After the picture was redone, the author said the copy was "a bit hard to follow": the red line appeared, but no line said "if someone is plain, only great kindness gets your notice", and the hospital part never said "a patient without diabetes was most likely there for the gallbladder". What needs adding is the missing step, not an explanation on every screen: the opening question and mid-video suspense are meant to hang for a while, as long as they pay off
- This time the running metaphor was named in the text the first time it appeared ("Your attention is a sieve:"); before, it lived only in a small label in the picture, and viewers couldn't connect it at the payoff
- An example that only asks the question leaves viewers on the question ("Great food, shabby place?"); this time each example got one line of reason ("Places bad at both closed long ago")
- Plain words next to numbers and terms: "unrelated" beside a correlation of 0.00, "one up, the other down" beside −0.83
- When the subject is an illusion, say "seems"; lines that flow easily tend to overstate ("every one of them had it" → "far more of them had it")
- Splitting one line into two screens evenly left the second half 2 seconds, gone before it was read; split by character count and time each screen with the reading speed above
